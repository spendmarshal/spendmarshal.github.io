How it works

1

Sits beside your model gateway

A hook in your LiteLLM proxy asks the firewall before each call and reports the real cost after it. Nothing is rerouted through anyone else.

2

Every agent gets a credential

Each credential carries a budget, a rate and the models it may use. A sub-agent's credential is cut from its parent's and can only be narrower, never wider.

3

Approval above your line

A call over your threshold waits for a named person to approve it, and the approval itself is signed.

4

A statement your client can verify

Every decision, approval and call becomes a signed, hash-chained receipt. At month end each client gets a statement of what its agents spent and who approved what, and anyone can verify it offline with an open specification and a short, standard-library Python verifier.

Questions

Why not just use the budgets in LiteLLM or my gateway?

Keep them as a backstop. They cap spend. This adds delegation that only narrows, a named approver above your threshold, and a record someone outside your company can verify.

Does any of my data or traffic go to you?

No. It runs on your infrastructure, and model calls go from your proxy to your providers as before.

Does it hold money or keys?

No. For model spend nothing moves; it decides, records and asks for approval. Your provider keys stay where they are.

Can it govern agents that pay for APIs directly?

Yes, optionally: it already supports the x402, MPP and L402 payment protocols, with the same policy and the same record.

What happens if the firewall is down?

The hook will let you choose: block calls until it is back (the default), or let them through and record the gap.
